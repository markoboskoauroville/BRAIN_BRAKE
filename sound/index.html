Scene 10:00 – 0:14

THE MYSTERY

Open road under flat overcast, a thin crowd, then absolute suspension. The freeze is the sound event, not the sprint.

Verified references, open and listen

Oceanmynoise.net/NoiseMachines/oceanNoiseGenerator.php

White Noisemynoise.net/NoiseMachines/whiteNoiseGenerator.php

Calibrate the crowd bed against this, then bury it.

Also worth hearing, find these by name in the catalogue

  • Stormy Weather — pressure without rain

The decision

At 0:07 everything stops. This is the most important sound decision in the film. Cut every layer at once, leave one held tone and one breath, and let the audience notice the silence before they notice the boy.

Seven rules for this mix

These matter more than the sources.

  • One sound per moment. Decide which single sound the audience is meant to hear and let it be alone or nearly alone. A full track feels professional and is exhausting, and nothing can rise if everything is present.
  • Build in layers, then remove. Everything not carrying meaning comes out. Loud films are made by adding, intense films by cutting.
  • Silence is a department. Ninety seconds of disciplined sound is what makes one second of true silence the loudest moment in the film. It goes under the most important line and is defended in the mix.
  • Bridge every transition. Six worlds in two minutes. Let the next world arrive a few frames before its picture does, or the cutting reads as channel hopping.
  • The voice stays constant. Dry, close and unprocessed in every world, so the audience has one thread to hold across all of them.
  • Room tone under everything. It does more for believability than any effect, and it is recorded on the shoot day or never.
  • Machinery becomes heartbeat. The rhythm established as machinery in scene two returns as a pulse in scene six. The ear connects them instantly and never announces that it has.
