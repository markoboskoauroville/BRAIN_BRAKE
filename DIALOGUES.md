# EVERY LINE IN THE FILM

Baba, 4.9.2026. One place for all of it, because until now the dialogue lived in
four: the catalogue against the frames, `dialogue_scene19` per shot, the recorded
takes, and the guide clips. Four copies drift, and three of them already had.

**Two people speak.** MANAN, the boy, played by Manan Periwal in real footage.
And VIVEKA, who was Coach Brain until 4.9.2026. Viveka is discernment, telling
the real from the unreal, which is the faculty he actually exercises, and the
Katha Upanishad uses the word for what drives the body-chariot.

**Nobody else has a line.** There is no voice-over and no narrator in the film
itself. The narration that exists is on the website, briefing the animator, and
it is not in the picture.

## 3. THE SCENE AS IT STANDS. VIVEKA'S ROOM

He arrives at the top of the lift. One room, one chair, and a man who has been
expecting him.

    VIVEKA   Ah. There you are. I was expecting you.

    VIVEKA   Before we begin. Raise my right arm.
    MANAN    I am trying.
    VIVEKA   I know.
    MANAN    I can't. Why can't I move it?
    VIVEKA   Try now.
    MANAN    That was you. It was never my arm at all.
    VIVEKA   The arm is always yours. The permission is mine.

**The whole idea of the film, done as a party trick instead of a lecture.** The
arm must genuinely not move: animate the effort, not the failure.

## 4. THE TWO THEORIES, ON THE HOLOGRAM

Manan never writes anything. He thinks it and Viveka puts it in the air, because
Viveka can read him. Then Viveka answers by visualising his own.

    MANAN    Nineteen twenty three. Hill puts runners on a treadmill and
             measures everything they do. His answer is simple. Fatigue is in
             the muscle.

    MANAN    You run. The muscle uses up its oxygen. The fuel is spent. And when
             the tank reads empty, the body stops.

    VIVEKA   Pulse, breath, temperature, water, sugar. I read all of it, the
             whole time. That is what every one of those panels you walked past
             is for.

    VIVEKA   And I ask one question with it. Can we carry on safely?

    VIVEKA   When the answer starts to look like no, I slow you down. Not
             because anything has run out. Because of where it is heading.

    MANAN    Heading.

    VIVEKA   I do not wait for the tank to empty. I watch how fast it is
             emptying, and I stop you while there is still enough left to get
             you home. So no. Fatigue is in the brain, not in your legs. Up
             here. And it decides long before anything is actually in danger.

    VIVEKA   When I stop you, the fuel is still there.

    MANAN    It was. I saw it. The sugar cellar was full. The fat store was
             barely touched. The fibres were not torn.

    VIVEKA   No. They never are.

    MANAN    So the wall...

    VIVEKA   Is a number I chose. Carefully. To keep you alive.

    MANAN    Then it can be a different number.

### What the boards say, and when

Three lines on each display, and **they do not arrive together**. Each appears
under the line being spoken, the way a highlighter runs across a sentence, so
the display fills as the argument is made and the third line lands on the last
word.

| MANAN'S BOARD | VIVEKA'S ANSWER |
|---|---|
| FATIGUE IS IN THE MUSCLE | FATIGUE IS IN THE BRAIN |
| THE FUEL IS SPENT | THE FUEL IS STILL THERE |
| THE TANK IS EMPTY | THE WALL IS A NUMBER |

Read the left down and then the right and you have the whole film in six lines.

## 5. RETIRED LINES

Written, recorded in some cases, and no longer in the film. Kept because a line
that was cut for a reason should not come back by accident.

    MANAN    It's back. It just came back.
    VIVEKA   It never went anywhere. I simply stopped signing for it.
    MANAN    But I was taught it works the other way. Nineteen twenty three.
    VIVEKA   And when you collapsed on that road. Was the tank empty?
    MANAN    No. There was some left.
    VIVEKA   There is always some left.
    VIVEKA   I read the heat, the distance, how this went last time, and I
             decide how much you get.
    VIVEKA   It decides when to stop.
    VIVEKA   Now you understand what I do.

**Why they went.** *There is always some left* was replaced by *No. They never
are*, which says the same thing and lets Manan get there first. The long
telemetry line was replaced by *pulse, breath, temperature, water, sugar*, which
is shorter and, unlike the original, correct. *Now you understand what I do* was
cut because the film should not tell the audience it has finished explaining.

## 6. STILL TO BE WRITTEN

**THE KEY IS A CHARACTER AND NOBODY SAYS THE WORD.** It hangs round Viveka's
neck, it is the door he came through, and it is the only gold in the film, and
the word does not appear once in the dialogue above. Baba, 4.9.2026: the word
should recur through the encounter, *the key is this, the key is that*, and the
hologram should carry it once as a line of its own. Not written yet.

**Manan's goodbye.** He leaves and says nothing. If he says anything it has to be
written before the next recording session.

**The conclusion.** One frame, no lines, and the beat has never been dictated.

## WHERE THESE LIVE, AND THE ONE RULE

The lines are attached to the FRAMES they play over, in `catalog.json`, and
everything else is generated from that: the storyboard prints them under the
picture, the card pages print them in full, and the guide cues speak them.

**If a line changes, change it there and nowhere else.** The same text existed in
four places for weeks and three of them said *distance* long after the boards
said *sugar*. This file is a reading copy, not a source: it is written from the
catalogue and should be rewritten from it, never edited on its own and copied
back.
