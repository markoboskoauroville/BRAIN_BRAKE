# SCENE 2, THE REVERSAL

**0:14 to 0:22.** The most important eight seconds in the film, and the ones Kristijan should be given
the most hours for.

---

## WHAT HAPPENS

The sprint does not cut. It comes back. Time begins to run the other way, slowly, and the camera
starts to travel around the runner's head while it does. Sweat that had fallen climbs. A drop on the
jaw rises along the wet track it made, reaches the temple, and goes back into the skin. His face
un-forms, intent easing back toward surrender. Somewhere in the middle of it he registers that
something is wrong, and that is the only moment in the film where the runner knows he is being looked
at.

It is not a freeze. Nothing stops. Everything runs backwards at about a fifth of speed while the view
orbits, and the orbit is what tells the audience that this is no longer their world.

---

## WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY

**A miracle shown wide looks like an effect. Shown on one drop of sweat it looks like a fact.** There
is no wide shot in this scene. Everything that matters is happening inside this man, and the only
things we can photograph of it are the physical manifestations on his surface: sweat, breath, skin,
the eye.

Time reversal has one cheap version and one expensive one. The cheap version is a body running
backwards, which is a hundred years old and belongs to silent comedy. The expensive version is
ordinary physics quietly disobeyed at a scale small enough to believe. Water does not climb. When it
climbs on a face we already care about, the audience does not think about a technique. They lean in.

**The reference is the Matrix orbit, and the reason it worked is worth naming.** A ring of cameras
fired at once, which is a simple trick. What made it a landmark was not the trick but the permission
it gave: for a few seconds the audience was allowed to walk around a moment that in life passes too
fast to see. That is exactly what this scene is for. Scene 1 is too fast to feel. Scene 2 slows it
enough to feel it, and then hands it to Manan to explain.

**He feels it, and this is deliberate.** The audience is already inside him from scene 1. If the
reversal is mechanical, they watch an edit. If he is surprised, they are surprised with him, and the
strangeness lands in a body rather than on a screen. It arrives late in the scene, not at the start,
because physical wrongness first and human reaction second is the order that convinces.

---

## THE THREE FRAMES

**2A, the run taken back.** Approved and stored. The runner drawn four times along one path, ghost
grey at the left where he is finished and staggering, firm dark graphite at the right where he is at
full sprint. Read right to left it is the sprint being undone. It carries the **3** of the number
ladder.

**2B, the drop that climbs.** The insert. Skin filling the paper, one drop of sweat travelling upward
against gravity along its own drying track, the trail closing behind it. No whole face, no context, no
horizon. This is the frame that has to be perfect, because it is where the audience decides whether
to believe the scene.

**2C, the eye.** The orbit arrives at the eye, and reflected in the wet curve of it is a small figure
in a caped coat and a deerstalker, raising a magnifying glass. Manan's first appearance in the film is
a reflection inside the man he has come to examine. The wide shot is implied and never shown, and the
handoff to scene 3 is made without a cut to anywhere.

---

## FOR THE ANIMATOR

The whole scene runs at roughly a fifth of forward speed, and the orbit is continuous, never stepped.
The single most important motion in the eight seconds is the drop in 2B: it must move slowly enough
that the eye can follow it and never so smoothly that it reads as a graphic. Water hesitates. Give it
the hesitation.

The sound of the scene is the reverse of the sound of scene 1, and the breath is the last thing to
turn around.

---

## FOR THE GRADE

The paper is at its warmest cream here. This is the low point of the light before it starts to climb
toward gold in scene 7 and white in scene 8.
