# SCENE 2, THE REVERSAL

**0:14 to 0:26.** The most important eight seconds in the film, and the ones Kristijan should be given
the most hours for.

---

## WHAT HAPPENS

The sprint does not cut. It comes back. Time begins to run the other way, slowly, and the camera
starts to travel around the runner's head while it does. Sweat that had fallen climbs. A drop on the
jaw rises along the wet track it made, reaches the temple, and goes back into the skin. His face
un-forms, intent easing back toward surrender. Somewhere in the middle of it he registers that
something is wrong, and that is the only moment in the film where the runner knows he is being looked
at.

It is not a freeze. Nothing stops. Everything runs backwards at about a fifth of speed while the view
orbits, and the orbit is what tells the audience that this is no longer their world.

---

## WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY

**A miracle shown wide looks like an effect. Shown on one drop of sweat it looks like a fact.** There
is no wide shot in this scene. Everything that matters is happening inside this man, and the only
things we can photograph of it are the physical manifestations on his surface: sweat, breath, skin,
the eye.

Time reversal has one cheap version and one expensive one. The cheap version is a body running
backwards, which is a hundred years old and belongs to silent comedy. The expensive version is
ordinary physics quietly disobeyed at a scale small enough to believe. Water does not climb. When it
climbs on a face we already care about, the audience does not think about a technique. They lean in.

**The reference is the Matrix orbit, and the reason it worked is worth naming.** A ring of cameras
fired at once, which is a simple trick. What made it a landmark was not the trick but the permission
it gave: for a few seconds the audience was allowed to walk around a moment that in life passes too
fast to see. That is exactly what this scene is for. Scene 1 is too fast to feel. Scene 2 slows it
enough to feel it, and then hands it to Manan to explain.

**He feels it, and this is deliberate.** The audience is already inside him from scene 1. If the
reversal is mechanical, they watch an edit. If he is surprised, they are surprised with him, and the
strangeness lands in a body rather than on a screen. It arrives late in the scene, not at the start,
because physical wrongness first and human reaction second is the order that convinces.

---

## THE THREE FRAMES

**2A, the run taken back.** Approved and stored. The runner drawn four times along one path, ghost
grey at the left where he is finished and staggering, firm dark graphite at the right where he is at
full sprint. Read right to left it is the sprint being undone. It carries the **3** of the number
ladder.

**2B, the spray that comes back.** The insert. The runner's jaw and cheek close, and off the skin a
fine spray of small sweat droplets hanging in the air, dozens of them, the nearest ones touching down
and being drawn back in, one or two still connected to the surface by a thread of water. Skin already
dry where they have returned.

One large drop was tried and abandoned. A single drop cannot say which way it is going, because the
eye assumes water falls, and the shape of a moving thing never carries its direction. The direction has
to be in what it has done to the surface behind it. A spray also gives the scene the thing a single
drop cannot: many small round surfaces, which is what scene 2 needs next.

**2C, the boy on the other side of the water.** Rebuilt. Not sweat. At the water station the runner
takes a bottle and tips it over his head, and it is in those flying drops that Manan first appears.

Sweat carrying a face was faintly unpleasant, and it was also the wrong element. See `elements.md`.
Water is compassion, and the whole story turns on somebody stopping for a man nobody else stopped for,
so the element and the meaning are the same thing and neither is ever said.

He is not a silhouette any more and not a tiny figure. **His face fills the nearest drop, pressed close
to the curve, looking out at us.** A drop of water is a lens, so the curve magnifies him, and what the
audience sees is a boy on the far side of a magnifying glass looking back through it. For one second
the observer and the observed change places and we are the specimen. That is the film's argument about
angles, made without a word.

This also finally agrees with the model rather than fighting it. Manan renders as a photograph when he
is large, single and in the foreground. A face filling a drop is exactly that. Every previous failure
came from asking for him small, or repeated, or both.

Behind that near drop, more water in the air. In those the face appears only as **fragments**, part of
an eye, an edge of a cap, a piece of a mouth, drawn rather than photographed. A complete tidy portrait
inside a small shape reads as a sticker. A fragment reads as a reflection.

## KRISTIJAN, THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT NOTE IN THE SCENE

**The approved frame does not contain the effect. You have to put it there.** Nano Banana cannot
produce refraction at all. It composites, it does not transform, so it will place a face inside water
but it will never bend, crop or displace anything according to a curved surface. Two runs proved it and
a third was not spent. `V4_2C` is therefore a composition and a mood, not a finished optical idea.

What is in the approved frame and should be kept: the teardrop shape, the boy's real photographic face
inside it, the drawn faces in the other drops, the open cream paper, the scale relationship.

What is missing and has to be built: **the water has to behave like his magnifying glass.** Right now
it is a container. It must become a lens. Four things do that, and all four are relationships rather
than textures, which is exactly why the still could not carry them.

**One, the image overflows the rim.** A magnified face does not fit. We should see brow to upper lip and
no more, cut off by the edge of the water. Headroom inside a lens is proof there is no lens.

**Two, something continuous crosses behind and is displaced.** The ruled finish line already runs across
every frame in this film. Outside the drop it is thin and straight. Where it passes behind the drop it
is thick and bowed and stepped out of line at the edges. This one detail does more than everything else
combined, and it costs nothing because the line is already there.

**Three, the centre is clear and the image bends toward the rim.** A sphere is sharp in the middle and
compresses at the edge. His eyes sit near the centre, his cheeks stretch wide and then pinch away.

**Four, a bright ring inside the edge and a darker band outside it.** That is total internal reflection
and it is what makes water look like glass rather than like a hole.

**And the shape has to become the tool.** The drop should end the shot as a lens with a firm rim,
because that is the shape of the brass magnifying glass he raises in scene 3. The audience never has to
be told. They meet the circle first as water and then as his instrument, and the association is already
made when he lifts it. In the approved still that association is not there yet. It is the single thing
most worth your hours in this scene.

**One warning from the failed attempt.** Do not let the drop become a perfect circle while a whole face
sits centred inside it. That combination reads as a fishbowl or a space helmet, and it is funny rather
than strange. The teardrop escapes it. If the shape rounds toward a lens at the end of the shot, the
face must be overflowing the rim by then, so the helmet never has a chance to form.

## FOR KRISTIJAN, THE SHOT THAT CANNOT BE DRAWN AS A STILL

The frame you are given is a hint. The shot is this.

The camera is inside the spray, among droplets of sweat hanging in the air off the runner's jaw,
travelling slowly forward and around at the same time, the same orbit that has been running since the
start of the scene. Time is going backwards at about a fifth of speed, so the droplets are drifting
**toward** the skin rather than away from it, and as each one reaches the surface it is drawn in and
the skin behind it goes dry.

Every droplet is a lens, so every droplet carries an image of the same boy, and as the camera moves the
image inside each one turns with the surface, so we see him from a different angle in every drop at the
same instant. In the nearest drop his face is close and clear and looking straight out at the audience.
In the others he is smaller and rougher, a shape more than a portrait.

That is the whole point of the shot and it is worth the hours. **One view of a thing is never the
truth of it.** The film is about a measurement taken from one position in 1923 that was correct,
careful and not the whole shape, and this shot says so without a word, eleven seconds before anybody
mentions science.

The count should feel like hundreds. In the near field, three or four droplets big enough to read the
shape. Beyond them the spray goes to specks, and specks are enough, because the audience completes a
pattern they have already understood.

The face in the near drop is his real face and it is the only place in scene 2 where it appears. Every
other drop keeps him rough and small. Do not cut to him in the world. He is still only in the water
until scene 3. The first sight of his face is scene 3, when he walks in at
human height and stands beside the man. If his face appears here that entrance is spent.

The last beat of the shot is one droplet reaching the skin and going in, taking its silhouette with it.
Then the pull back begins.

---

## FOR THE ANIMATOR

The whole scene runs at roughly a fifth of forward speed, and the orbit is continuous, never stepped.
The single most important motion in the eight seconds is the drop in 2B: it must move slowly enough
that the eye can follow it and never so smoothly that it reads as a graphic. Water hesitates. Give it
the hesitation.

The sound of the scene is the reverse of the sound of scene 1, and the breath is the last thing to
turn around.
