## THE ROOM, AND WHY IT IS DESIGNED RATHER THAN FOUND

It is a real bedroom in a real flat in Bangalore, and it should look like one. Not a studio, not a
neutral wall, not a set. The judges are looking for a student in his own life, and the room is half of
that.

**What it is.** A boy's room in an Indian apartment. Painted wall, a little uneven. A window with a
grille outside it and thin curtains, the light coming through hard and warm. A ceiling fan. A wooden
study desk against or near the window, a chair with a cushion, a bed with a printed cover. A shelf of
schoolbooks with a few trophies or a cricket bat. A pinboard or wall with posters, some of them sport.
A charging cable, a water bottle, a pair of shoes on the floor. Tube light overhead that we will not
use.

**It must be tidy but not styled.** Lived in. If it looks art directed the film loses the one thing it
is buying with this location.

## THE THIRD LAYER, WHICH IS WHERE THE OLD LANGUAGE GOES

Marko's instruction, and it is the right place for it. Everything from the dense symbolic version
survives, but it moves to the **background** and it never competes with the boy.

**The five elements, in his room, without a single one being pointed at.**

    earth    the desk, the books, the weight of the boy in the chair
    water    a water bottle on the desk. It is there from the first frame and
             it is the object that saves the runner in the story
    fire     the warm hard sunlight through the grille, and later the desk lamp
    wind     the ceiling fan turning through the whole film, and the curtain
    ether    the empty wall above his head, and the space the drawn things
             appear in

**The threads that carry over.** The number twenty seven on the runner's vest. The lever that stops
short. Round shapes echoing across the film, the fan, the mug, the lens of the laptop camera, the drops.
Light travelling from hard afternoon at the start to soft evening lamplight by the end, so the two
minutes feel like a single afternoon of a boy working something out.

**And the rule that keeps it in its place.** None of this is ever the subject of a frame. If the audience
notices the fan, the frame is wrong. It works the way a bass line works, which is that you feel the
song differently and you could not say why.
