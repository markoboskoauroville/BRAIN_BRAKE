# SCENE 5, VERSION SEVEN. THE EXPERIMENT

Was `THE TRICK`, 1:14 – 1:30, six frames, all drawn, Manan absent.
Now `THE EXPERIMENT`, 1:14 – 1:36, eight frames, and he is in five of them.

---

## WHY IT CHANGED

The old scene reported a study. A cyclist raced a ghost of his own best ride, the researchers made the
ghost two percent faster without telling him, and he beat it. Good science and a good story, and Manan
was nowhere in it.

**He cannot run that experiment on himself, because it needs somebody to lie to him.** The moment he
sets the ghost faster he knows, and knowing is exactly the variable being tested. So the study stays,
short and drawn, as the thing he watches.

**What he can test is the third finding, the one the film has never dramatised.** Mindfulness training
increased how long athletes could keep going, with no measurable change in the body. Nothing about the
legs changed. What changed was how hard it felt.

That is a boy, a bicycle, and his own attention. No laboratory, no researcher, no trick.

**And it plants the gesture that ends the film.** He closes his eyes and breathes, here, in the middle
of an ordinary afternoon, and something gives. In 8.1 he stands in white and does the same thing, and
the audience already knows what it means without a word being spoken about it.

---

## THE SHAPE

A, he watches. B, the idea lands and he gets up. C, he tests it and it works.

The turn is at 5.4, and it is not a discovery, it is an appetite. The scene is not about whether the
theory is true. It is about a boy who has just learned something and cannot sit still.

---

## THE FRAMES

### 5.1 A cyclist and a ghost
**Drawn.** A clean laboratory, not a clinical one. The cyclist on a stationary bike facing a screen with
a translucent version of himself on it. Short, this is a citation, not a scene.
**Manan.** Voice over. *"So they let a cyclist race a recording of his own best ride."*

### 5.2 YOU and BEST
**Drawn.** The screen, plainly lettered, his ghost ahead of him. A researcher's hand turns a dial. +2% on
the monitor. One conspiratorial click, nothing said.
**Manan.** Voice over. *"Except they made the ghost two percent faster. And they didn't tell him."*

### 5.3 He passes his own best
**Drawn.** The cyclist goes by, spent and astonished, the ghost dissolving behind him.
**Manan.** Voice over. *"He beat his own maximum. Which means it was never his maximum."*

### 5.4 The idea lands
**Live action. SETUP A.** Manan at his desk, the drawn scene finishing on his laptop. He sits back. A beat
of stillness while it arrives, then he looks off toward the door, and then he is already getting up,
chair scraping, out of frame before the shot is ready to end.
**Manan.** No line. The whole beat is on his face and then on the empty chair. *This is the turn and it
is played as appetite, not as understanding.*

### 5.5 Out on the road
**Live action. EXTERIOR. NEW SETUP.** Manan riding a real bicycle on an ordinary road near his house,
flat out, morning light. Shot from a following vehicle or a fixed wide as he passes. This is the
recording he is about to race.
**Manan.** Voice over, dry and a little amused at himself. *"So I tried it."*

### 5.6 Racing himself
**Live action. SETUP E.** Back in his room on the stationary bike, and the footage from 5.5 playing on the
laptop in front of him. He is riding hard alongside his own recording. Three layers: him near, the
screen middle, the window behind.
**Manan.** Voice over. *"Same legs. Same road. Same everything."*

### 5.7 The wall
**Live action. SETUP E, closer.** He is level with the recording and he cannot get past it. Teeth, sweat,
the effort completely real. He is at the place where the film began, the place where the runner's head
went back.
**Manan.** Nothing. Let it run. *The sound carries this: breath, chain, the room.*

### 5.8 Eyes closed
**Live action. SETUP E, close.** He closes his eyes. Two seconds of nothing at all, just breath. Then his
legs pick up.

**Drawn, briefly, inside his legs.** Three or four frames of muscle fibres lighting, the same clusters as
6.4, arriving early and only for a moment.

Then his eyes open, he is ahead of the recording on the screen, and he laughs.

**Manan.** Voice over, over the closed eyes. *"Nothing about my legs changed."*
Then, after he opens them: *"Only what I believed they had left."*

## WHAT THIS COSTS

**One extra setup and one extra location.** Venkatesh shoots 5.5 outside on an ordinary road, thirty
minutes, and it produces the footage that plays on the screen for the rest of the scene. Nothing else in
the film needs an exterior, so this is the only time the shoot leaves the house.

**Setup E is new but it is the bike setup we already have.** 5.6, 5.7 and 5.8 are one lighting state and
one camera position, wide then closer then close. Half an hour.

**The drawn work goes down, not up.** The old scene was six drawn frames. The new one is three drawn
frames, one brief insert of fibres reusing the 6.4 artwork, and five live action frames. Scene 5 becomes
cheaper to animate.

**The film runs six seconds longer.** 1:14 to 1:36 against 1:14 to 1:30. Scene 7 can give back four
seconds without harm, it is the most patient scene in the film. The remaining two come out of scene 2,
where the factory establishes itself faster than it needs to.

---

## WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN

**No metaphysics.** He closes his eyes and breathes. Nothing glows around him, nothing lifts him, no
light comes down. If the shot needs an effect to work, the performance is wrong and the effect will not
save it.

**No superpower.** The pick up in 5.8 is small and believable, the difference between giving up and not
giving up. He beats a recording of himself by a bike length, not by a street.

**He does not explain what he did.** He never says the words attention, or breathing, or meditation. He
closes his eyes, it works, he laughs. The audience arrives at it on their own, and that is worth more
than being told.

**The reserve is still his.** Nothing is given to him from outside. He had it the whole time and the
film has been saying so since scene 3.

## WARDROBE, DECIDED 13.8.2026

**Hoodie for 5.4 and 5.5. T shirt for 5.6, 5.7 and 5.8.**

He watches at his desk in the grey hoodie, gets up and rides the road in it, comes back sweating, takes
it off, and races the recording in a t shirt. The change reads as time passing between the outdoor ride
and the indoor session, which is exactly the gap the scene needs and never states.

Nobody rides a stationary bike indoors in a hooded sweatshirt, and the film loses nothing: the hoodie is
his identity in scenes 1, 3, 4 and 8, which is where the two worlds law actually depends on him being
recognisably the same boy.

**This means the first bike frame, `V7_5_1_bike.jpg`, is off model.** It has him on the stationary bike
in the hoodie. Its job was to prove the screen composite and it did, so it can be regenerated in a
t shirt whenever it is convenient. Not urgent.

## THE FIBRES ARE A CUT, NOT AN OVERLAY

Settled when the 5.8 plate came back. Nothing is composited into his leg and nothing glows on him.

The drawn fibres are their own brief insert, three or four frames of the existing `6.4` artwork arriving
early, cut in between his eyes closing and his eyes opening. Drawn stays drawn, photographed stays
photographed, and the boundary between them is a cut rather than a blend.

The 5.8 plate carries the whole beat on his face with nothing added to it. If an effect is needed to
make that shot work, the performance is wrong.
