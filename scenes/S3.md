# SCENE 3, THE ARRIVAL

**0:26 to 0:33.** New scene. The film is now nine scenes, not eight.

---

## WHAT HAPPENS

The view snaps outward. What filled the paper a second ago was one drop of sweat, and in a single fast
pull it becomes skin, then a face, then a shoulder, then the whole man, small and alone on open paper
with the finish line still crossing behind him. He is held mid stride where the reversal left him.

Manan walks in from the side and stops beside him, and for the first time in the film he is at human
scale, the same height as the runner, a real photographed boy standing next to a drawing. He does not
look at the man's face. He looks at his calf.

He raises the magnifying glass to the leg. The lens fills the paper. We go through it.

---

## WHY THIS SCENE EXISTS AT ALL

Scene 2 has no wide shot in it by design, which leaves the audience with an accurate feeling and no
geography. That debt has to be paid before the film can go anywhere, and paying it is a job worth
seven seconds of its own rather than a job squeezed onto the end of the reversal.

It also gives Manan a proper entrance. He was glimpsed in scene 2, inverted, dozens of times, inside
water. Here he is a whole person standing on the same ground as the man he has come to examine. A
character introduced twice, first as a hint and then as a fact, is stronger than a character
introduced once, and the audience does the connecting themselves.

**The snap is the point.** A slow pull back would be a camera move. A fast one is an event, and it
lands as relief after eight seconds of held breath. It is the exhale of the first act.

## THE THREE FRAMES

**3A, the pull back.** Done. The runner suddenly small on open paper with graphite streaking outward
from him toward every edge. There is no blur in this film. A pencil does not blur, it streaks.

**It was not generated, it was built.** Nano Banana inherits subject scale from its references and
cannot be talked out of it, and it cannot organise a field of streaks around a point either. So `V4_3A`
was assembled in the container from `V4_1C`, scaled to a third of frame height and set on open paper
with the streaks and the ruled line drawn in. Continuity is therefore exact rather than approximate,
because it is the same figure and not a redrawing of him. Any pull back or push in later in the film
should be made the same way.

`assets/V4/attempts/2B_a1_face_hanging_sweat.png` still serves as an in-between of this move, since the
pull passes through that framing on the way out.

**3B, the two of them.** They face each other. Manan comes in from the **right**, close to us and
filling the height of the paper, and the runner hangs in the air at the **far left**, cropped by the
border, still facing right into the frame as he has in every frame of the film. Manan is looking down
at the calf, not at the face.

**The size question, settled.** They are at their true proportions, a fourteen year old boy and a grown
man, and the difference in how much paper each of them occupies comes from **depth rather than height**.
Manan is nearer. That solves what the first attempt got wrong, where he was scaled to the man and
started reading as a small adult.

It also puts him where the tool can render him: large, single and in the foreground. His entrance and
the model's one reliable condition happen to be the same thing.

This is the empathy frame of the scene, and it comes from the fact that a boy has walked up to a man
that nobody else stopped for.

**3C, into the lens. The calf is not in the frame, the calf IS the frame.** Recomposed 10.8.2026 from
Marko's sketch, which is the authority on this frame. Three attempts were rejected before it and all
three failed the same way, a glass held up over open paper, which reads as somebody demonstrating a prop
rather than examining a man.

**Three layers, and nothing else in the picture.**

**One, the leg, behind everything.** The calf rises the full height of the paper, its back edge running
up the left side and its front edge up the right, so the muscle crosses almost the whole width and bare
cream paper is left only in two narrow slivers at the far edges. No knee, no ankle, no foot, no shoe. At
that distance a leg stops being a leg and becomes a landscape, which is exactly right for the moment
before we go inside it.

**And it is seen from the SIDE.** This is the thing the sketch says most clearly and the thing that has
been got wrong. Not the front of the leg and not the back of it. We have come at this man from the side,
so the swell of the calf muscle bulges out along the **left** edge of the picture and the long straighter
front of the shin runs down the **right**. It is not symmetrical and it is not an anatomy plate. Asking
for an anatomical study without saying profile produces the textbook posterior view every time, both
heads of the muscle, straight on, which is a diagram of a leg rather than a man being looked at.

**And it is an anatomical drawing, not a leg.** Muscle, tendon and fascia as an anatomist would draw
them. No skin, no body hair. Attempt four put fine hairs across a flayed muscle study and the
contradiction is the first thing the eye catches.

**Over the anatomy, the intact leg as a translucent contour.** A fine light outline of the whole calf
laid over the muscle study like an X-ray plate over a diagram, so two depths read at once, the shape of
the man and the machinery inside him. The muscle is darker and detailed, the skin line is faint. This is
also the thesis of the film drawn rather than said, the difference between the outside of a person and
what is actually happening inside them. **Proved on attempt five, first time, and it does not need to be
fought for again.** See `worlds.md`.

**Two, the glass, lying on the muscle.** Large, a third of the picture across, plain thin brass rim, a
turned brass collar and a dark wood handle running down to the right and out of sight behind the cap. It
rests on the muscle rather than hovering over it, and graphite surrounds the circle on every side. **The
calf frames the lens.**

**Three, the cap, closest to us, and only a hint of it.** The camera is above and looking down, so what
enters the bottom of the frame is the **peak of the deerstalker and very little else**, a small shape
breaking the bottom border. Not the crown, not the ear flaps, not half the picture. No face, no shoulder,
no arm, no hand.

**Sharp, and it casts a shadow.** Softness was tried and it is the wrong idea. Everything is in focus and
the cap throws a directional shadow across the pencil lines, which stay readable inside it. That shadow
is what makes the photograph and the drawing occupy one space, and it is the mechanism the whole film
runs on. `worlds.md` carries the law.

**Why the hand had to go, and this is the craft lesson of the frame.** See `nanobanana.md`, 10.8.2026. An
arm is a property of a figure and the model poses arms the way arms are posed, raised and out to the
side. Where the glass is pointing is a relationship between two objects, and relationships are the one
thing the model cannot hold. So the arm won three times and carried the glass off the leg with it. Remove
the arm and the glass has nowhere to be except on the muscle. The constraint and the composition arrived
at the same answer again.

**And inside the circle is not a bigger calf. It is the laboratory.** Faint and pale, as though it were
under the skin rather than printed on the glass. A cluttered desk buried in loose paper, more paper across
the floor, anatomy sheets pinned on the wall behind, one lamp, and a man with wild white hair sitting with
his chin in his hand, stuck. He is not teaching anybody. He is alone with a problem he has not solved,
which is the only way scene 5 can work, because a man who is lecturing cannot be surprised and a man who
is thinking can.

**What the room is actually for.** This is the establishing shot of the scientist, and the arc that starts
here is not about a theory being correct. It is about how much work a discovery costs. Paper everywhere,
experiment after experiment, trial and error, a brain worked to the edge of burning. He finds his answer
by the end of scene 4, and the audience should feel what it took. A film that shows only the conclusion
teaches a fact. A film that shows the paper on the floor teaches what science is.

**The technical reason the room is there at all.** Whatever is in the lens is the first inch of scene 4,
and it has now become the only version that can be made, because the model cannot magnify anything.
Magnification is a transformation. Putting different content inside a circle is an arrangement, and
arrangement is the one thing it does reliably. The constraint and the story arrived at the same frame.

The circle also pays off scene 2. The audience met a round lens as water with a boy inside it, and here is
the same circle as his instrument with a room inside it. Nothing needs to be said.

**For the grade.** The brass is the first gold in the film and gold is reserved for scene 7, so the rim is
held close to graphite here and let go properly gold only when the key appears.

**The loop is open on this frame.** Marko has set aside the two run limit for 3C specifically, because it
is the hinge between the boy and the science and it is worth the credits. Run, look, change one thing,
run again.

## THE THREAD THAT NEEDS A DECISION

The old rule was that the magnifying glass descends through the film in opposition to the rising key.
The key has moved to the Coach's chain and the leg is now below and the head above, so a monotonic
descent no longer matches what the film does.

Proposed replacement: **the glass falls to the leg and then rises to the head**, which is the film's
own movement, real below and invented above, and it is put down in the last scene. Marko to confirm.

## FOR THE ANIMATOR

The whole scene is one continuous move: out fast, hold, then in fast. Two accelerations with a
stillness between them, and the stillness is where the audience meets Manan. Do not fill it.
