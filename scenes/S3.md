# SCENE 3, THE ARRIVAL

**0:26 to 0:33.** New scene. The film is now nine scenes, not eight.

---

## WHAT HAPPENS

The view snaps outward. What filled the paper a second ago was one drop of sweat, and in a single fast
pull it becomes skin, then a face, then a shoulder, then the whole man, small and alone on open paper
with the finish line still crossing behind him. He is held mid stride where the reversal left him.

Manan walks in from the side and stops beside him, and for the first time in the film he is at human
scale, the same height as the runner, a real photographed boy standing next to a drawing. He does not
look at the man's face. He looks at his calf.

He raises the magnifying glass to the leg. The lens fills the paper. We go through it.

---

## WHY THIS SCENE EXISTS AT ALL

Scene 2 has no wide shot in it by design, which leaves the audience with an accurate feeling and no
geography. That debt has to be paid before the film can go anywhere, and paying it is a job worth
seven seconds of its own rather than a job squeezed onto the end of the reversal.

It also gives Manan a proper entrance. He was glimpsed in scene 2, inverted, dozens of times, inside
water. Here he is a whole person standing on the same ground as the man he has come to examine. A
character introduced twice, first as a hint and then as a fact, is stronger than a character
introduced once, and the audience does the connecting themselves.

**The snap is the point.** A slow pull back would be a camera move. A fast one is an event, and it
lands as relief after eight seconds of held breath. It is the exhale of the first act.

---

## THE THREE FRAMES

**3A, the pull back.** The runner suddenly small on open paper, streaks of graphite radiating outward
from him toward every edge. There is no blur in this film. A pencil does not blur, it streaks, and a
snap zoom drawn as radiating hatching reads faster and cleaner than any softened version of it.
`assets/V4/V4_2B_v1_rejected.png` is reinstated here as an in-between of this move, since the pull
passes through exactly that framing on the way out.

**3B, the two of them.** Manan standing beside the runner, the same height, a real photograph next to
a pencil drawing, both full figure on open cream paper. He is looking at the calf, not the face. This
is the empathy frame of the scene, and it comes from the fact that a boy has walked up to a man nobody
else stopped for.

**3C, into the lens.** The brass magnifying glass raised to the runner's calf, the glass large in the
frame, the muscle enlarged and clear inside the circle and the paper ordinary outside it. Whatever is
visible inside the lens is the first inch of scene 4.

## THE THREAD THAT NEEDS A DECISION

The old rule was that the magnifying glass descends through the film in opposition to the rising key.
The key has moved to the Coach's chain and the leg is now below and the head above, so a monotonic
descent no longer matches what the film does.

Proposed replacement: **the glass falls to the leg and then rises to the head**, which is the film's
own movement, real below and invented above, and it is put down in the last scene. Marko to confirm.

---

## FOR THE ANIMATOR

The whole scene is one continuous move: out fast, hold, then in fast. Two accelerations with a
stillness between them, and the stillness is where the audience meets Manan. Do not fill it.
