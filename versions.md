## WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED AND WHY

**V1 to V3 was tightening.** Same film, better told.

**V3 to V4 was Marko.** The dialogue came off, the cartoon became graphite, and the film became a piece
of visual storytelling with a thesis: the limit is real and somebody set it. It is the strongest thing
made on this production and twenty seven frames of it exist.

**V4 to V5 was research.** The competition scores four things equally and one of them is whether the
viewer understood. Judges are reading the student. A previous winner recommends the student be on screen
for at least forty percent. V4 had eight seconds of a hundred and twenty, so the best thing about it,
the silence, was the thing that would lose.

**V5 to V6 is Neha, and she is half right.** V1 and V3 have the better hook and V5 opens with a boy
talking, which is weaker television. She is wrong about the fix, which is a corner inset or a voice over,
because that is a narrator and it is the exact shape the rubric penalises.

## THE OPEN QUESTION FOR NEHA

Two ways to make V6 and they are genuinely different films. Marko will board whichever she prefers.

    A  THE GHOST IN THE FRAME
       The drawn world runs as it did in V4, and Manan appears inside it,
       walking through the frozen race, small against the crowd, examining
       the leg. He is present as a figure but he speaks little.
       Keeps the visual power. Risks him reading as a device.

    B  THE BOY IN HIS ROOM
       Manan is in his own bedroom explaining, as in V5, but the visuals he
       reaches into are as strong as V4. The pop up book, the anatomy, the
       control room, all of it, coming to him and around him.
       Keeps the rubric safe. Risks the opening being quieter.

**Marko's own reading, recorded here because it will matter later.** B is closer to what wins, A is
closer to what he most wants to make, and the real answer is probably B with an A opening: begin in the
mystery with no explanation at all, then find the boy already inside it.
