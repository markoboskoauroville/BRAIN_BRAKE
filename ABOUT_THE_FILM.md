# THE BRAIN BRAKE, five ways

**The same film described at five depths.** Written 19.8.2026, from the film as it stands: fifty
frames, eight scenes, one boy, one drawn world.

---

## FOR A FIVE YEAR OLD

A man is running a very long race. He is so tired he cannot go on. He stops.

Then, right near the end, he suddenly runs fast again.

A boy called Manan wants to know where that fast came from. He has a magnifying glass, and when he
looks through it, he goes into a different world made of pencil drawings.

In that world he finds a big room with lots of clocks and buttons. Someone is sitting there. It is a
friendly brain with arms and legs and a tracksuit, and he is the boss of the running.

The brain says: I am not stopping you. I am looking after you. I always keep a little bit of running
saved up, in case you need it.

So the boy goes home and rides his bicycle. And he goes faster than he ever has. His legs were the
same. He just believed there was more.

At the end he tells us: next time somebody runs fast at the end, do not think about their legs.
Think about their brain.

## FOR A TEN YEAR OLD

For a hundred years people thought that when you get tired, it is your muscles giving up. They run
out of fuel, so you slow down, so you stop. A scientist called A. V. Hill said this in 1923 and
everyone believed it.

But there is a problem. When scientists tested runners at the exact moment they said *I cannot go
on*, the muscles could still do far more. There was plenty left. So something else was stopping
them.

The film is a detective story about who.

Manan presents it. He is fourteen and he is real, a photograph, and he walks around inside a world
that is drawn in pencil on cream paper. That is the film's trick: **anything real is a photograph,
anything that is an idea is a drawing.** The muscle factory is a drawing. The old theory is a
drawing. Manan is real.

He finds a door at the back of the factory that nobody opens. Behind it is a control room, and in it
is Coach Brain, a cartoon brain in a tracksuit with a whistle and a small gold key.

Coach Brain explains himself. He watches your heart, your breath, your temperature, your water and
how far is left. He asks one question over and over: *can we keep going safely?* When the answer
starts to look risky, he makes you feel tired. Not because you are broken. Because he is protecting
you.

Manan tests it. He races his own best time on a bicycle, and he beats it. His legs did not change
that day. What changed was what he believed his legs had left.

Then Coach Brain gives a little more, and inside the leg the muscle fibres light up, more of them
than before. He did not make new energy. He gave permission.

The film ends: the limit is a setting, not a wall.

## FOR A FIFTEEN YEAR OLD

This is a two minute film for a science competition, presented by someone your age, and the whole
thing is built on one argument.

**The claim.** Fatigue is not a report from your muscles that they have failed. It is a decision made
in your brain, in advance, to hold something back before you get hurt. The model is called the
Central Governor Theory, proposed by Tim Noakes in 1997, and it is one influential explanation rather
than settled fact. The film says so on screen.

**The evidence, in order.** Hill's 1923 model says fatigue starts in the muscle. But muscle biopsies
taken at the moment of exhaustion show fuel still present and force still available. So exhaustion
arrives while the capacity is still there. Something decided.

Then the cyclists. Riders were set against a recording of their own best previous ride, without being
told, and beat it. If their first ride had been their true maximum, that would be impossible. So it
was never their maximum.

**The structure.** It is a detective story, deliberately. A mystery you cannot explain, an old
explanation that gets stamped CASE CLOSED, a door at the back nobody opens, a suspect who turns out
not to be a villain, and a confession. Coach Brain is never the enemy. That matters, because the
science is not that your brain is holding you back out of weakness, it is that your brain is running
a safety margin.

**The form.** Fifty still panels cut in rhythm, like a comic strip, rather than continuous animation.
Manan is photographed and composited onto drawn paper, and he casts a real shadow onto it, so he is
inside the drawing rather than pasted over it. The transition between worlds is a magnifying glass:
the picture travels through the lens.

**What it is really about**, underneath the physiology. It is about the difference between *I can't*
and *I am not allowed to yet*. One is a wall. The other is a setting.

## FOR SOMEONE NEW TO FILMMAKING

**The problem the film had to solve.** The competition limit is two minutes and a film that runs over
is not judged at all. The first honest assembly of this script was 2:47. So every structural decision
here is a subtraction, and the form emerged from the constraint rather than from taste.

**Why it is a comic strip.** Continuous animation is expensive per second. A held panel is not. So
fifty still frames, some on screen for as little as ten frames, let a two minute film carry fifty
images instead of fifteen. Then it turned out the form suited the subject, because a comic strip is
how a fourteen year old thinks about a mystery.

**The one rule the whole look is built on.** Photograph what is true, draw what is thought. Anything
real, the presenter and the evidence, is a photograph. Anything conceptual, the theory, the factory,
the coach in your head, is graphite on cream paper. The two layers are not kept apart: the
photographed boy sits on the paper and casts a real shadow onto it. Where the worlds touch, the
shadow does the work.

**The shooting reality.** One day, one fourteen year old, a grey backdrop, a white void, his own
bedroom, a real blackboard and one quiet road at dawn. Nine setups. No runner, no crowd, no factory,
no coach. Everything else arrives in Zagreb afterwards and is built around him.

**The layer discipline.** Every frame is delivered as a background plate with nobody in it, a
character as a transparent PNG with a solid body, and a composite showing what it must look like. The
animator moves them independently and gets parallax. The presenter's keyed footage drops in as its
own layer.

**Where the time actually goes.** Not where a beginner expects. The shoot was one day. The writing,
the timing, the art direction, the retiming from measured audio, and the sound design are months.
Sound alone is roughly half the film and does not exist yet.

**The thing worth stealing.** There is one file that describes all fifty frames: what is in each one,
who speaks, what they say, how long it holds, and whether that duration is measured from real audio or
estimated from a word count. Every document is generated from it. The presenter's acting book, the
camera guide, the animator's packages, the read through. Nothing is typed twice, so nothing can drift.

## FOR SOMEONE WHO MAKES FILMS

**The formal problem.** A two minute hard cutoff, a fourteen year old presenter with no acting
training, a single shooting day in Bengaluru, an animator in Zagreb, a director in Croatia who never
attended the shoot, and a subject that requires three separate pieces of evidence to land. That is
the brief, and every choice in the film is an answer to it.

**The compositing thesis is the argument.** Photographic layer for the empirical, graphite for the
theoretical, and a real cast shadow at the seam. It is not a style, it is the film's epistemology
made visible: *this is a measurement, that is a model.* The magnifying glass as the only transition
between them is the same idea, a device for looking closer at evidence.

**Coach Brain is the film's real structural risk and its best decision.** Personifying the central
governor invites teleology, the brain as a homunculus deciding things, which is exactly the
misreading the theory attracts in the literature. The film manages it three ways. He is a coach, not
a commander, so his authority is protective rather than absolute. His dialogue is functional rather
than emotional: *heart rate, breath, temperature, water, distance,* a list of inputs. And the film's
closing formulation, *it didn't make new energy, it gave permission*, is careful to keep the physical
capacity fixed and locate the change entirely in the regulation of it. That single line is where the
science lives or dies.

**The cut is doing argumentative work, not just rhythmic work.** Scene two builds the old model and
stamps CASE CLOSED. Scene three is a single reversal, *tested the moment they gave up, the muscles
could still do far more*, placed immediately after the stamp so the audience feels the contradiction
before it is explained. The cyclist study is held back to scene five and used as confirmation rather
than as the reveal, and Manan's own bicycle test follows it, which converts an experiment into a
demonstration and gives a young audience someone to identify with. That ordering is the difference
between a lecture and a film.

**Timing methodology.** Frames are timed in frames, and a duration is either measured from a real
recording or flagged as estimated from a word count, per frame, in the source file. Estimates were
wrong by more than two seconds on a single line. The whole film retimes when the presenter's audio
lands, which changes structure, so the estimate flag is a first class piece of data rather than a
note. Every derived document carries it through.

**The unresolved problem, honestly.** The presenter is not projecting. He speaks inward, nasally,
without support, aimed at nobody. The current plan is imitation learning: generate every one of his
lines with a directed synthetic voice and let him copy the placement and the energy rather than the
timbre, then take the reference away before the take. It is a defensible method and it has an obvious
failure mode, a boy doing an impression, which is why the reference is removed before recording and
why an alive take beats a correct one.

**What it is about, if it is about anything.** A boy discovers that the thing stopping him is not
made of muscle, and is not an enemy, and can be renegotiated. Everything else is physiology.
