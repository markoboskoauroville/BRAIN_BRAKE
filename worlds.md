# worlds.md

**The law of the two worlds, and how they become one.**

A standing rule document, written 10.8.2026. It governs every frame in which a photograph and a drawing
share the paper, which is most of the film. Read it before drawing, shooting or animating anything that
has Manan in it. It is also the source for the section of Kristijan's animation brief on the same
subject, and Venkatesh has to shoot for it on 18.8.

---

## 1. THE LAW

**Everything drawn is the inside of the problem. Everything photographed is the person who came to look
at it. The film is the moment those two stop being separate.**

Nobody says this and nothing in the film explains it. It is carried entirely by whether the photograph
and the drawing are lit by the same light.

Until now the two worlds have been layered. A photographic boy has been placed on top of a drawn world
and the seam is visible, and where the seam is visible the audience reads a composite. The fix is not to
soften the boy. It is to let each world leave evidence on the other.

---

## 2. THE FIRST CUE, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS: THE SHADOW

**Manan casts a shadow onto the drawing.**

Light falls on him and on the paper at the same time and from the same direction, so where he enters the
frame the sheet goes dark under him. His shadow crosses the pencil lines and the pencil lines stay
visible inside it, because a shadow darkens paper rather than covering it.

That single detail does the whole job. A shadow is proof that two things occupy one space. Without it he
is pasted on. With it he is standing on the drawing, and the audience concludes, without ever thinking
it, that the drawing is a place.

**The shadow is soft edged and directional, not a halo.** A contact shadow ringing the object is what a
compositor does when there is no light. A cast shadow with a direction is what happens when there is.

**For the camera, 18.8.** This is the one lighting decision the whole method depends on. Manan is shot
with a single dominant key from a consistent side, hard enough that his shadow has a shape, and there is
a clean pass or a matched angle that lets the shadow be reconstructed on the paper later. If the light on
the day is flat, every frame in the film loses this and it cannot be added back honestly.

---

## 3. THE SECOND CUE: THE SHADOW IS DRAWN

Once the shadow exists it can do something better than being correct.

**A photographic object casting a shadow made of pencil hatching is the union stated in one image.**
Half of the object belongs to the real world and the thing it throws onto the paper belongs to the drawn
one. Nobody notices it deliberately and everybody feels it.

Use it sparingly, and let it grow. Early in the film the shadows are soft and photographic. As the film
goes on they become more clearly hatched, until in the last scenes his shadow is drawn in the same hand
as everything else. The two worlds converge over two minutes rather than in one shot.

---

## 4. THE THIRD CUE: THE LINE CROSSES OVER

A pencil line that runs off the paper and continues across the brass rim of the glass, or around the
edge of the cap, says that the same hand made both. One line, once or twice in the film, never as a
pattern.

The reverse also works. Where a photographic edge touches the paper, let the pencil contour be drawn
around it, as though somebody traced him.

---

## 5. THE FOURTH CUE: THE PAPER IS ON HIM

The cream of the paper is in the light on his skin and in his shadows. He is not neutral daylight sitting
on a warm sheet. Graded so that the paper's own tone is in him, he stops being a cutout.

This costs nothing and happens in the grade.

---

## 6. TRANSPARENCY, AND HOW THE ANATOMY IS DRAWN

**An anatomical drawing is an anatomical drawing.** No skin, no body hair, no attempt at a living leg.
Muscle, tendon, fascia, the lines an anatomist would draw. Body hair on an écorché is the mistake that
gave the frame away.

**Above the anatomy, the outline of the real leg, translucent.** A fine light contour of the intact calf
sits over the muscle study like an X-ray plate laid over a diagram, so the eye reads two depths at once:
the shape of the man and the machinery inside him. The muscle is the darker and more detailed of the two.
The skin contour is the fainter.

This is worth saying plainly because it is also the thesis. **The film is about the difference between
the outside of a person and what is actually happening inside them.** The X-ray is that sentence drawn.

The model can do this, because transparency and what sits over what are arrangement, not transformation.
See `nanobanana.md`.

## 7. OBJECTS ARE CHARACTERS

An object that appears more than once and is drawn differently each time is not a thread, it is a
mistake repeated. The magnifying glass, the key and the dial each carry meaning across scenes, and
meaning does not survive a change of shape.

**Every recurring object gets a sheet with multiple angles, exactly like a character.** One sheet is one
credit and saves five, and without one the object is also the first thing the model drops from a busy
frame, because it has no identity to hold on to.

**The proper word for it is a model sheet**, from the Disney and Warner studios of the nineteen thirties,
and for an object specifically a **prop model sheet**. The rotation through several angles is a
**turnaround**. A drawing that departs from the sheet is **off model**, which is the verdict word. All the
sheets bound together with the style law and the palette are the **bible**. In live action the same
instinct produces the **hero prop**, the version built to survive a close-up, plus continuity stills shot
on the day. Use these words with Kristijan and with Venkatesh, because the loose word gets the loose
thing. The reasoning is in the book, part twenty seven.

**Which world an object belongs to.** Everything Manan carries is photographed, because it arrived with
him: the deerstalker, the coat, and the brass magnifying glass. Everything belonging to the runner, the
scientist and the Coach is drawn. So the glass is a real object lying on a drawing and it throws a
shadow, and what you see through it is pencil. A real instrument showing a drawn interior is the film's
argument in one object.

## 8. FOR KRISTIJAN

This document is the source of the animation section on the two worlds, and it goes to him in Croatian
with the brief. The three things he has to be told without ambiguity:

**One, the shadow.** Wherever anything photographic sits on the paper it darkens the paper and the pencil
lines stay readable through the darkness. This is not optional decoration, it is the mechanism of the
whole visual idea.

**Two, the transparency.** The anatomy is a diagram and the skin is a ghost over it. Two layers, two line
weights, the inside darker than the outside.

**Three, the convergence.** The two worlds start separate and end joined, and his hours should go into
making that gradual rather than sudden. By the last scene the boy and the drawing are lit by one light.

Style stays his. What is described here is what happens and why, not how to draw it.
