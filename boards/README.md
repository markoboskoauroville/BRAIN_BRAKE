# boards

**Private. Not for the crew.** Storyboard sheets made to compare versions of the film against each
other, one sheet per scene, six rough thumbnails per sheet.

Opened 11.8.2026 at Marko's request, after the scene 5 board settled a story question in seconds that
prose had failed to settle in paragraphs.

    SCRIPT_0    Manan's original, six scenes. A broadcast marathon, a Sherlock entrance,
                a chalkboard, a muscle factory, Mission Control, Coach Brain, a phone
                battery, a final sprint, a handshake. Dialogue throughout.

    V4          The film as it stands. Nine scenes, no words anywhere, graphite on
                cream, one photographic boy, a laboratory, a lens, a lever.

The point is not to decide that one is better. It is to see, side by side and on paper, **what was
gained and what was lost**, because a film that has been rebuilt four times can quietly lose something
that was working in version one and nobody notices until it is printed.

Boards carry no lettering. The tools either invent words or omit them, and captions are not needed to
read a thumbnail.

## THE PANEL LAYOUT, SET 11.8.2026

Marko's example is filed as `boards/LAYOUT_gemini_panel_example.jpg`. **This is the standing layout for
boards from now on**, and it replaces the six up sheet as the unit of work.

**One panel per image, and a caption block ruled underneath it, not inside it.**

    the drawing        a single thumbnail in a hand ruled rectangle, rough pencil
    SCENE              the scene number, on its own ruled line
    ACTION             one short sentence of what happens in the panel
    DIALOGUE           what is said, or (No Dialogue)
    PANEL              the panel number, right aligned on the same line as DIALOGUE

**Why it is better than the six up sheet.** A single panel can be reordered, replaced, dropped or
regenerated on its own without touching the other five, and the caption is under the picture where an
animator reads it rather than in a document he has to hold alongside. It is also the standard board
layout every crew in the world already knows, which matters more than any preference of ours.

**And `DIALOGUE: (No Dialogue)` is not a formality on this film.** It appears on every panel of all
twenty seven and it is the loudest thing on the page. An animator flicking through sees immediately that
nothing is ever explained out loud, which is the single hardest thing to convey about this version in
prose.

**When Gemini or any other tool writes its own captions into the image, they are ignored.** The drawing
is cropped out and the caption block is set as text on the web page, where it is correct, searchable,
selectable and can be fixed without regenerating anything.
