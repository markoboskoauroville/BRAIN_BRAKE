# storytelling.md

**The laws underneath the film. Why a scene works, not how it is made.**

Sits beside `nanobanana.md`, `documents.md` and `codebox.md`. Those three are craft. This one is the
reasoning, and under the rule in `documents.md` section 6 it is allowed to be long, provided it stays
interesting and never repeats itself.

Read this before writing a scene, a prompt, a shot list or a brief. Every choice in the film should
be traceable to something here.

---

## 1. THE FIRST LAW: EMPATHY BEFORE CURIOSITY

**A scene earns feeling before it asks a question.**

This is the reason scene 1 works, and it was nearly lost by being left unwritten.

Look at what the opening actually does. It does not begin with the Central Governor Theory. It does
not begin with a question about fatigue. It begins with a man whose head is tipped back and whose
hands have fallen open, and the audience feels him before they wonder about him. Only then, once
they are already inside his body, does the film ask where the sprint came from.

Reverse that order and the film dies politely. An audience given a question before a feeling will
answer it intellectually, decide the answer is interesting, and remain outside. An audience given a
feeling first will chase the question as though it were their own, because by then it is.

**The order, in every scene, without exception:**

1. **Feel.** Someone we care about is in a state we recognise.
2. **Ask.** Something happens that does not fit.
3. **Learn.** The scene answers, or refuses to answer, and hands us to the next scene.

Curiosity is fuel, but empathy is the tank. Fill the tank first.

**How to check a scene against this law.** Cover the dialogue and the captions and look only at the
frames. If a stranger looking at those three images would feel something before understanding
anything, the scene is built correctly. If they would understand first and feel later, or never, the
scene is an explanation wearing a scene's clothes and it must be rebuilt.

## 2. WHY THIS FILM PARTICULARLY

Most science films fail at exactly this point, and they fail for an understandable reason: the maker
already finds the idea fascinating, so they forget that fascination is a destination and not a
starting position.

This film has a harder version of the problem. It has two minutes, a competition jury, real
published science to honour, and a fourteen year old presenter. Every one of those pressures pushes
toward explaining early. All of them must be resisted for the first ten seconds.

The subject helps, because the subject *is* feeling. Fatigue is not a reading on an instrument. It
is an experience everybody has had and nobody enjoys. That is a gift: the audience arrives already
holding the raw material of the film in their own bodies. The opening simply has to touch it.

## 3. THE THESIS, WHICH IS NEVER SPOKEN ALOUD

The leg is science. Everything above it is emotion, and emotion decides how much there is.

Happy and there is more energy. Sad and there is less. Frightened and the reserve closes. Safe and
it opens. The brake is not in the muscle.

The film argues this by structure rather than by statement. Scene 3 is a laboratory because that
part of the answer is measurable. Scene 6 is imagination because that part is not, and pretending
otherwise would be a lie about the science. The audience learns the rule without being taught it:
**below is real, above is invented, and the invented part is where the limit actually lives.**

Nobody in the film ever says this. If a line of dialogue starts to say it, cut the line.

## 4. THE SHAPE OF A SCENE

Every scene carries the same curve as the whole film, which is why every scene is drawn three times.

- **A, introduction.** Establish the state. This is where empathy is bought.
- **B, peak.** The turn. Not the loudest moment, the moment something changes. It usually falls a
  third or two thirds of the way through, never in the exact middle, because the exact middle feels
  mechanical.
- **C, resolution.** The release, and the handoff to the next scene.

A scene without a turn is a description. A scene whose turn arrives at the very start has no floor
to turn from. A scene whose turn arrives at the very end has no room to land.

**Scene 1 as the worked example:**
1A a man finished, hands open, alone on empty paper. Empathy.
1B his eyes open and fix on something. The turn, and it is recognition rather than decision, because
decision is still one beat away.
1C he sprints, released, and the question is now the audience's own.

## 5. STORY MEDICINE, THE SIX PRINCIPLES

These come from therapeutic storytelling and they are why the film leaves a person lighter than it
found them. They are structural, not decorative.

**Externalise the difficulty.** The problem is never inside the person as a flaw. It is a character,
a room, a lever, a dial. A boy watching this should meet his own tiredness as something with a shape,
sitting in a chair with a coffee mug, rather than as evidence that he is weak.

**No blame.** Nobody in this film is at fault. Hill is not wrong, he is incomplete, and he is drawn
with respect. Coach Brain is not a villain, he is doing his job well. The runner is not failing. The
moment anything in the film has a culprit, the audience starts defending themselves instead of
listening.

**The resource is already inside.** Nothing arrives from outside to save the runner. The reserve was
always there. This is the difference between a film that inspires and a film that makes somebody feel
they need something they do not have.

**Never state the lesson.** The moment the film says what it means, the audience stops feeling it and
starts evaluating whether they agree. Let the images carry it. If a caption is doing the work of a
frame, the frame is not finished.

**Plant before it is needed.** Everything that matters at the end is already present at the
beginning, unnoticed. The number, the line, the light. When the ending lands it should feel
inevitable rather than clever, and inevitability is made entirely of things planted early.

**End in safety, with a transfer.** The film closes calm and open, and it hands something back to the
person watching. Not a message, a possibility. They should leave with the sense that this is about
them, without ever having been told so.

## 5b. THE ROOM ANSWERS THE MAN

Added 11.8.2026, from Marko, out of a mistake that turned out to be right.

The flame in `4C` came back twice the height of its own chimney because an intensity was named without a
limit. As a still it is an error, because a lamp does not do that. **As animation it is a beat**, because
the inanimate things in a frame are allowed to follow the energy of the person in it. The flame leaps
when he sees it. That is not physics, it is the oldest trick in hand drawn animation, and it is invisible
to an audience while doing all of the work.

So both frames are kept. `V4_4C` is the settled state and `V4_4C_peak` is the top of the leap, and the
direction to the animator is that the flame flares on the instant of realisation and comes back down
over the following second.

**The general law, and it is worth applying across the film.** Where a character turns, let one thing
near them turn with them. The flame here. The paper on the desk lifting when he comes forward. Whatever
is nearest and lightest.

**And the reason it belongs in this film in particular.** Fire is will, and the film has already decided
that the limit lives above the muscle and not inside it. A room that responds to a man is the same
argument told in furniture: the state of the person changes the state of the world around them. It says
the thesis without saying anything.

**One caution.** Used more than three or four times in two minutes it becomes a tic and the audience
starts watching the technique. Save it for the turns.

## 6. THE THINGS THAT BREAK IT

Each of these has been tried on this project and abandoned. They are listed so they are not tried
again.

**Opening on the science.** The old version 1 began with the theory. It was accurate, it was clear,
and it was inert.

**Two jobs in one place.** The old leg carried both Hill's science and the metaphor world, and did
neither well. One space, one job.

**Mocking the wrong theory.** An early draft stamped CASE CLOSED on Hill. It is a cheap laugh, it
makes the film look like it is scoring points, and a jury that knows the literature will mark it
down. The theory is presented as incomplete, which is both kinder and more accurate.

**Overstating certainty.** The Central Governor model is genuinely still contested. The film should
feel like it has found the answer, because that is what makes the release land, but one honest line
near the end protects it. A fourteen year old holding a strong view *and* knowing it is argued about
reads as more sophisticated, not less.

**Saying what the picture already says.** Every caption that describes the frame it sits under should
be deleted. See `documents.md`.

**Explaining before investing.** The general form of all of the above, and the one to watch for.
