# documents.md

**How every document on this production is written.**

Sits beside `nanobanana.md`. Read both before making anything. If a rule here is broken, the document
is wrong even if every sentence in it is true.

---

## 1. THE RULE

**You should feel the film, not read the film.**

A document about this film is a piece of the film. It moves the way the film moves: an emotional
hook first, then curiosity, then release. It does not explain the film in the tone of a report.

Anyone who reads the first pages and nothing else must come away **feeling** what we are making.
Not informed about it. Feeling it.

---

## 1b. THREE FRAMES PER SCENE, NOT ONE

**Every scene is drawn three times: introduction, peak, resolution.** Eight scenes, twenty four
frames. The word "hero image" is retired, because a single frame cannot show an arc and a scene
without an arc is not a scene.

Each scene carries the same energy curve as the film itself. Something is established, it rises to a
peak somewhere around a third or two thirds through, and it resolves. The peak is not always the
loudest moment; it is the moment the scene turns.

This is why the documents can be short. Three frames laid down the page tell the movement of a scene
without a paragraph explaining it. Show it, do not talk about it.

Frames are named by scene and position: 1A introduction, 1B peak, 1C resolution.

---

## 2. LENGTH

**Short. Then shorter.**

- **One page per scene. Two or three pages around it.** Eight scenes is a ten page document, not
  twenty five. Twenty five pages for eight scenes was wrong and will not happen again.
- **One image is worth a thousand words, so write the summary of those thousand words, not the
  thousand.** The frame is already on the page. Three or four sentences beside it, and stop.
- A page is a frame plus a few lines. If the lines run past the image, cut them.
- If a paragraph can go without losing the feeling, it goes.

---

## 3. WHAT NEVER GOES IN A DOCUMENT

Delete on sight:

- Recaps of conversations. Nobody reading needs to know what was discussed.
- "Next steps", "action items", "who will contact whom", schedules of calls.
- Anything about process rather than about the film.
- Repeating in prose what the image already shows.
- The same idea said twice in different words.
- Filler headings that introduce nothing.

A department head opens the document to find out what to make. Everything else is noise between him
and that.

---

## 4. WHAT EACH READER NEEDS

**The cinematographer** looks at the frame and at Manan in it. From the image he reads the lighting.
From two or three lines he reads the setup, the eyeline and what the moment is about. That is all he
needs and more will slow him down.

**The animator** needs the story of the shot in a handful of words, and the **emotion** he has to
produce. He is not making a diagram, he is making a feeling. Give him the feeling in one line and
the concrete facts in a few more.

**The client and the performer** need to feel the film in the first pages and to be able to say yes.

---

## 5. THE SHAPE OF EVERY DOCUMENT

Same arc as the film itself.

1. **The hook.** The first page states the problem the way scene one states it: emotionally, fast,
   no preamble, no contents page, no "this document describes". A person one step from stopping who
   suddenly sprints. Curiosity in ten seconds.
2. **The unrolling.** One page per scene, image and a few sentences, curiosity turning into
   understanding.
3. **The release.** The end of the document lands where the film lands. Light, not a summary.

The first eight pages are the whole film for anyone who stops there. Everything past page eight is
for whoever wants more.

---

## 6. THE ONE EXCEPTION, AND IT IS A REAL ONE

**Principles may be long.**

When the subject is the thinking underneath rather than the film's surface, write fully and well:

- composition
- colour and palette
- the number ladder and what the numbers carry
- light, and how it travels through the film
- metaphor, symbol and structure
- the science being explained

These may run as long as they are interesting. They are the reasoning behind every choice, and a
department head who understands the reasoning makes better decisions than one following an
instruction. But **interesting** is the condition: no repetition, no padding, no restating. Long
because there is a lot worth saying, never long because it is thorough.

Keep this material in its **own document**, not mixed into the scene pages. Somebody wanting to
shoot tomorrow morning should never have to wade through the theory of the palette to find his
setup.

---

## 7. THE THING THE FILM IS ACTUALLY SAYING

Every document should carry this without stating it:

The leg is science. Everything above it is emotion, and emotion is what decides how much you have.
Happy and there is more energy. Sad and there is less. The brake is not in the muscle.

Write the documents the way the film works. Science where the film is scientific, and feeling
everywhere else.

---

## 8. FORMAT

- Bilingual documents carry the language in the filename, `[ENG]` or `[CRO]`, immediately before the
  extension.
- One image per scene page, large. The image is the content, the text is the caption.
- No contents page on a ten page document.
- No signature block, no version history inside the document.
- Croatian for Croatian crew, English for everyone else.

## FILE NAMING FOR ANYTHING THAT GOES TO A PERSON

Set 11.8.2026 by Marko. **Every deliverable file starts with its language in square brackets.**

    [EN] 6 - v5 camera guide - Venkatesh.pdf
    [HR] 7 - v5 animatorski brief - Kristijan.pdf

**Why it is at the front and not at the end.** Marko reads these on a phone, in a list, at a glance,
and often in a hurry before forwarding one to somebody. The language decides who the file is for, so it
is the first thing that has to be readable. A suffix is invisible when the middle of a long name is
truncated, which is what phones do.

**The rest of the name, in order:** the number, the version, what it is, who it is for. Human readable
with spaces, because these are files people receive rather than files code reads.

**Where a document exists in two languages it keeps the same number**, so `[EN] 7` and `[HR] 7` are the
same brief for the same person. Kristijan gets Croatian and works in it, and the English exists so that
Neha, Manan or anybody else on the film can read what the animator has been told.

Internal repo documents are not renamed. This applies to `assets/pdf/` and to anything sent out.
