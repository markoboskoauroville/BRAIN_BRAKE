## THE TURN THIS SESSION TOOK

Marko's own words, kept because they are the reason everything downstream changed:

> I got carried away. I am a cinematographer and editor and my visual story is the most important thing
> to me. And then I realised, I am making my film, not Manan's film. He went from protagonist to guest in
> my visual story, and he was not even speaking.

**Version 4 is the visual peak and the exact point of the mistake.** Manan is on screen about eight
seconds of a hundred and twenty and does not speak. **The rubric wants the opposite.** Four equally
weighted criteria, illumination among them, the student judged rather than the film, and the winning
shape is always a student physically in the film explaining one idea in their own words. A former winner
recommends the student be on screen at least 40% of the time.

**Neha rejected versions five and six and went back to script one, Manan's original.** Her suggestion for
placing him was, her words, *"in a corner of the frame, or as voice over"*, which is the shape the rubric
penalises. She is the client.

---

## THE EXECUTION IDEA THAT CAME OUT OF IT

No acting, no costumes, no compositing Manan into drawn worlds. Three layers instead:

1. Manan in his own room, own clothes, one lighting setup, talking.
2. Small drawn details animated over him while he talks. An arrow, a number, a diagram, a brain that
   jumps in and vanishes.
3. When something needs showing, he drifts into memory. Cut, cut, cut, three or four **static** frames,
   two or three seconds, narration over them, then back to the room.

This kills the two most expensive things at once and reuses the 168 frames already made.
