# MANAN, THE RECORDING

**Notes toward the re-record. Not yet a method. Written 19.8.2026, to be worked out properly when
the recorder arrives.**

---

## THE PROBLEM, NAMED

Listen to the session recording and the same thing happens on almost every take.

**He is speaking inward.** The sound sits in his nose and in his chest and never leaves him. It has
no forward placement, so it arrives at the microphone as something overheard rather than something
said. It is nasal, it is low in energy, and it is aimed at nobody.

He is not doing anything wrong. It is what almost everyone does the first time a microphone is
pointed at them. You get quieter, you close down, you speak to yourself and hope it is enough.

**His mother heard it too, and she is right.** Through the whole session she keeps saying the same
thing: *each word, open your mouth please. Open your mouth. Central governor theory, open your
mouth.* That instruction is the correct one and it was given a dozen times. What it did not come
with is a demonstration, and you cannot copy an instruction. You can only copy a sound.

---

## WHAT WE ACTUALLY WANT FROM HIM

Not louder. **Outward.**

- The voice leaves his body and goes to a person. A real one, at a real distance, who has never
  heard this before.
- The mouth genuinely opens, so the sound comes out of the front rather than through the nose.
- Breath underneath the line, so it carries to the end instead of dying at the last three words.
- He is telling somebody something he finds interesting. That is the whole performance. Not acting,
  not projecting in the theatre sense. Telling.

---

## THE METHOD, AND THIS IS THE IDEA WORTH KEEPING

**Give him something to copy, not something to understand.**

The film already has six lines performed by Hume, and they are good: the coach is warm and open and
unembarrassed, and every word arrives fully formed. That is exactly what Manan's delivery is
missing.

So: **generate every one of his lines with Hume**, with the acting direction written the way it is
written for Coach Brain. He listens to his own line spoken the way it should sound. Then he says it.
Then he listens to himself against the model.

He is fourteen. Telling him to project means nothing. Letting him hear the difference between his
version and a full one takes about four seconds and he will hear it immediately, because everybody
does.

**The direction of the whole thing is worth saying out loud.** These voices were built from human
speech. Now a boy learns from them what a full, open, confident voice sounds like, and grows into
it. That is a good use of the technology and it is why this is worth doing properly rather than just
recording him again and hoping.

---

## THREE CAUTIONS FOR WHEN WE BUILD THIS

1. **He copies the placement and the energy, never the voice.** The Hume reference is a British
   adult. If he imitates the accent or the timbre it will sound like a boy doing an impression, and
   that is worse than what we have. Say this to him first, before he hears a single reference.
2. **Take the reference away before the real take.** Play it, let him try it, then record him without
   it in his ears. A performance made while copying sounds copied. He needs to have absorbed it and
   then done it himself.
3. **It is his voice that wins the competition.** The reference is scaffolding. If a take is more
   alive and less correct, keep the alive one.

---

## PRACTICAL, ALREADY DECIDED

Zoom H1essential on a Manfrotto Pixi, on something soft, never bare on a hard desk. Speak across the
microphone at about fifteen degrees, not into it. The room with the most soft things in it, a
bedroom rather than anywhere tiled.

One line per take, as on the shoot. Rolling through the resets. No counting down. Breaks every
twenty minutes. Take six is usually the one.

---

## WHAT IS NOT DECIDED

The order of the lines. Whether he hears all the references first or one at a time. How many passes.
Whether Neha runs it or Marko runs it over a call. That gets worked out when the recorder arrives.
