## 1. THE ONE IDEA THAT CONNECTS THE HOUSE TO THE BRAIN

A control room only means something if you can see what it is connected to.

So every room has a sensor in it, and out of every sensor runs a fine line of
light. The lines leave the rooms, join the ceiling of the corridor, and run the
same way he is walking. **Each room he passes adds its line to the bundle, so the
bundle thickens as he goes.** By the time he reaches the last hall, the ceiling
above the key door is a thick trunk of them, all pouring into it.

The audience works out where the door leads before it opens, and nobody has to
say a word. It also plants the line Coach Brain says later, *I read all of it,
the whole time*, so that when he says it, it is a confirmation rather than an
announcement.

**And the cables are not a metaphor.** They are group III and IV muscle
afferents: the thin sensory fibres that run from working muscle to the central
nervous system and report its metabolic state. Blocking them with fentanyl
demonstrably changes how hard a person can push, which is the experimental
backbone of the whole central governor argument. The film can draw them as
optical fibre and still be telling the truth.

## 2. THE ROOMS, AND WHY THESE SIX

Noakes built the theory by ruling the physical explanations out one at a time.
At exhaustion the runners had not run out of oxygen, had not exhausted their
muscle fibres, and had not used up their glycogen, fat or ATP. **That list is the
corridor.** Each room is where an obvious answer is supposed to live, and each
one is found still supplied.

| room | what it is | what its sensor reads | what he finds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The lungs** | ventilation | oxygen saturation, carbon dioxide | air still coming in |
| **The heart** | delivery | heart rate, stroke volume, blood pressure | still pumping, well within range |
| **The muscle** | the effort itself | contraction force, hydrogen ion, the afferents themselves | fibres whole, not torn |
| **The sugar store** | liver and muscle glycogen | blood glucose | the cellar still stocked |
| **The fat store** | the largest reserve in the body | free fatty acids | barely touched |
| **The furnace** | mitochondria | oxygen consumption, heat produced | lit, running, not starved |

Two more readings belong to the **corridor** rather than to any room, because
they are whole-body and not local: **core temperature** and **hydration**. Put
those on the wall panels between doors. Core temperature especially: rising heat
is one of the strongest things that makes a brain slow a runner down, and it
belongs everywhere rather than behind one door.

**The muscle room is the one that matters dramatically.** It is where he is most
certain he will find the answer, and it is where the cables are thickest, and it
still tells him nothing. That is the beat that sends him to the key door.

## 3. THE HIGH TECH, AND HOW MUCH OF IT

The house is grown, and the machine is fitted into it. Clay and cartilage, with
instruments set in. **The rule is that the architecture is organic and only the
INSTRUMENTS are engineered.** If the walls start looking machined the idea dies.

Sparingly, and in this order of frequency:

- **Sliding doors.** Two machined panels parting at the middle and withdrawing
  into the stone, a fine recessed groove down each face, a thin strip of light
  along each leading edge, a slim track at the threshold. No handles, no hinges.
  Automatic, because he is expected.
- **Optical fibre.** Fine bright lines leaving each room and running the ceiling.
  One or two out of a small room, a bundle by the end. This is the spine of the
  whole sequence and the only element that appears in every frame.
- **A status panel beside each door.** Small, flush, one or two lines of readout
  and a steady indicator. Never a screen full of numbers: a glance, not a
  dashboard.
- **A probe in each room.** One instrument, obviously placed there afterwards,
  touching the organ it measures. A cuff, a clip, a needle-thin line into the
  tissue. It should look like something a technician installed, not something the
  body grew.
- **One lift.** Somewhere in the middle of the corridor, a car and a shaft in the
  same machined language, because a body has levels and a house has to get
  between them. Used once, seen once, not explained.

**Star Trek is the right reference for the RESTRAINT rather than the look.** Those
sets read as advanced because almost everything is a calm surface and only a few
things glow. The moment every wall is a display it stops being a future and
starts being a shop window.

## 4. COACH BRAIN'S ROOM, AND WHAT HE ACTUALLY WATCHES

The room is the far end of the same cable. Every fibre in the house arrives here
and terminates in the wall of dials he sits in front of. **He should never have
to explain what the dials are, because we have just walked past every one of
their sources.**

What is genuinely on that wall, and all of it real physiology:

- heart rate, stroke volume, cardiac output
- ventilation rate, arterial oxygen saturation, carbon dioxide
- **core temperature**, the one he is most protective of
- blood glucose, and muscle glycogen remaining
- blood pH and hydrogen ion, which is acidosis
- plasma volume and osmolality, which is hydration
- and the afferent traffic itself, the thing the film has just drawn

The dialogue already on the boards, *pulse, breath, temperature, water,
distance*, is close to right. It should become **pulse, breath, temperature,
water, sugar**, because sugar is the one an audience already half believes in
and it is the one Noakes disproved most directly. Distance is not a physiological
signal and it is the weakest word in the line.

His answer to *can we carry on safely* is read off those, and his one honest
claim is the last line of the argument: **when I stop you, the fuel is still
there.** The corridor has already shown that to be true six times over.
